My general motivation for exploring this choice was my interest in music, 
particularly older music, and I was interested in seeing what factors influence the making of a popular song. 
The data, collated at MIT, consisted of taking almost 1000 hit songs from the 1980’s across all genres, 
and analyzing many of their different attributes, including tempo, duration, and time signature. 
I ended up further exploring my question of what key signatures were the most and least popular for these songs.


From my dataset, for every entry, I kept a total tally of how many songs were in each key, and extracted the value of column 6
(ie. I parsed each line to an ArrayList and took the value at index 6), 
which was a number from 0-11 corresponding to each of the 12 keys in music. 
I then increased the tally for that corresponding key, and once all of the data was collected, 
I calculated the total tallies for each key and which was most popular. 
After writing my code, I ran into a lot of error messages, 
but these were quickly solved by triple checking my file locations and making sure I was calling the correct pathname.

The data showed that the most popular key signature, with 159 songs, was C major, 
a result I was anticipating, as it’s the simplest key. The other two keys with over 100 songs apiece were G and A, 
which are good keys for both vocalists and guitarists. 
However, I’m not entirely sure of the validity of all of these songs–for example Jessie’s Girl, which is in D major, 
is listed as being in G instead, and with a set of 1000 entries, 
I wouldn’t be able to go through them all and manually check their correctness, 
especially the ones I don’t know. If I had more time, I’d examine the correlation between energy and danceability in songs, 
which was one of my other original questions.
